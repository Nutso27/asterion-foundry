# Religion-removal retcon — Ember Faith → Office of Human Continuance

*Archived from `lets remove the religious aspect of this. instead.md`
(Perplexity design chat, saved by Ryan). This is the source for the
retcon referenced throughout `lore/canon.md`, `lore/glossary.md`, and
`lore/gaps.md`. This chat is NOT cycle-tagged — treat it as a doctrinal/
worldbuilding decision layered onto the setting, not a dated in-story
event.*

## The decision

Ryan's stated intent, in his own words from the chat: the point isn't
that the Directorate is anti-spiritual for its own sake — it's that
"humanity put our faith into something tangible and real. Put it into
us as a species and what we can become, then through that belief we
will make it possible."

This replaces the entire Ember Faith / First Flamekeeper / Sisters of
the Ember / Undying Flame framing from the Command & Rank Structure v4
draft (see `command-and-rank-structure-v4.md`) with a secular-humanist
founding law.

## The Decree of Human Continuance

The Directorate's founding law (replacing the old faith-centered
foundation):

> Humanity shall not surrender its future to powers it cannot question,
> promises it cannot test, or authorities that place themselves beyond
> human judgment.
>
> We have crossed the dark not because we were chosen, but because we
> learned; not because we were saved, but because we saved one another.
> Every hull, harvest, archive, clinic, forge, and world made habitable
> is proof that the future is not granted. It is built.
>
> Therefore no faith, priesthood, oracle, cult, or claimed divinity
> shall stand above the human conscience, the evidence of reality, or
> the law made for human continuance.
>
> Let no one wait for a hand from the heavens. Let humanity take one
> another's hands, and make the heavens ours.

State motto options: **"No salvation is coming. Build it."** / **"What
humanity requires, humanity must make."**

The underlying ideology is called **Human Ascendancy**: humanity has the
capacity and obligation to rise above its vulnerable beginnings through
knowledge, solidarity, and deliberate construction — not supremacy over
others, not the claim that every human action is righteous.

## What the decree actually bans (and doesn't)

**Prohibits:** religious institutions exercising political/military/
legal/economic/scientific authority; clerical hierarchies claiming
obedience above civil law; prophecy/revelation overriding evidence,
medicine, education, law, or research; coercion, discrimination,
sacrifice, or inherited status justified by divinity; private armies,
compulsory tithes, secret property, or territorial rule by a religious
institution; any person, office, bloodline, machine, cult, or
institution claiming to be the literal embodiment of Humanity.

**Does NOT ban:** private grief, personal reflection, art, family
tradition, memorial practice, philosophy, non-coercive cultural custom,
or unanswered existential questions. The point is separating private
meaning from public authority, not erasing inner life.

| Branch H — The Ember Faith | Branch H — **The Office of Human Continuance** |

| Shrine Warden / Celebrant / Acolyte | World Curator / Civic Advocate / Archivist-Aide / Candidate (see rank ladder below) |
| Shrines and cathedrals | **Continuance Halls** — combined archive/school/museum/assembly space/emergency-coordination center/memorial; one of the first permanent civic buildings on a new colony |
| The divine flame / "Undying Flame" | **The Human Spark** — the state seal's flame motif kept, but reinterpreted as humanity's capacity to understand/make/remember/choose/continue, belonging to the species, protected by no god, not eternal on its own |

**First Steward of Continuance rank ladder** (per the source chat):
First Steward of Continuance (Council) → Dominion Steward → Sector
Steward → World Curator → Civic Advocate → Archivist-Aide → Candidate.

**Constitutional rule established alongside this:** "No citizen, office,
bloodline, machine, or institution may be named the embodiment of
Humanity" — this directly forecloses the Grand Director sliding into
worship even without an official state faith, and is flagged as good
dramatic tension if a later Director tries to violate it.

## The Vigil's mandate, reworked

Old: investigates "heresy against the Faith" among other things.
New: investigates religious revivalism and cult activity as an ongoing
counter-insurgency problem, alongside its existing remit (corruption,
xenos contact, rogue technology, threats to the Grand Director's rule).
This preserves the "two secret police that distrust each other" dynamic
between the Vigil (unaccountable, ideological) and the Bureau
(procedural, compliance-focused) — just recast around human
self-determination rather than religious purity.

## Deliberate design choice: religion can still emerge later

This is explicitly **not** meant to make the setting permanently
incapable of religion. A future faith, cult, or genuinely unexplained
phenomenon can emerge organically later — especially on isolated
colonies, after catastrophe, or around technology/practices that produce
real, measurable effects nobody yet understands. The Directorate's
response to such a thing should be **contested**, not automatically
villain-coded on either side (the Collegium wants to test it, the
Chancellery wants to regulate it, the Bureau treats it as a security
risk, the Vigil judges it against the decree, ordinary frontier citizens
may just be glad of the help). A **"Continuance Test"** (a 7-question
legal/political framework) is proposed for judging any such movement:
does it claim authority above evidence/law/judgment; does it demand
obedience through fear or promised salvation; can members freely leave/
dissent/access outside help; does it run private armed power or a
parallel chain of command; does it measurably improve welfare/survival/
cooperation; are its effects testable/replicable rather than only
available through its leaders' authority; does it serve humanity or
demand humanity serve it.

**Bottom line for the archive:** the Ember Faith is retired. Nothing
about the current setting should reference the First Flamekeeper, the
Sisters of the Ember, or the Undying Flame as active institutions.
First Steward of Continuance is the correct current seat name — this
matches the existing project brief and code-facing docs, which already
only ever mention "First Steward of Continuance," never any of the
older names.
